# Module I: Foundations & Diagnostics

## Section 1: General Overview & Foundations

### Course Overview & Core Pedagogical Pillars
The **IELTS Band 7+ Complete Prep Course** is structured around reverse-engineered examiner rubrics. Reaching Band 7.0–8.5+ requires moving beyond passive conversational English and executing rigorous academic discourse structures under strict timing conditions.

1. **Rubric-First Calibration**: Every strategy directly targets official IELTS criteria:
   - **Writing**: Task Achievement / Task Response (25%), Coherence & Cohesion (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%).
   - **Speaking**: Fluency & Coherence (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%), Pronunciation (25%).
2. **Unified Organization Method**: Consistent, repeatable formulas for every writing task and speaking response, eliminating uncertainty on exam day.
3. **Keyword & Paraphrasing Engine**: Recognizing synonym pairs, morphological shifts, and antonyms under intense time constraints.
4. **Distractor Neutralization**: Proactively spotting and discarding examiner traps (qualifiers, absolute statements, negative reversals like *however*, *on the other hand*, *actually*).

### Strategic Study Schedules
- **4-Week Intensive Schedule**: 3–4 hours/day. Alternates Writing Task 2 + Speaking on Days 1, 3, 5 with Reading + Listening + Writing Task 1 on Days 2, 4, 6.
- **8-Week Standard Schedule**: 1.5–2 hours/day. Dedicated single skill deep dive per week with cumulative full-length timed review every weekend.
- **Master Rule**: Never attempt timed mock simulations until achieving 80%+ accuracy in un-timed tactical application drills.

## Section 2: General Information & Exam Architecture

### Objective vs. Subjective Assessment
- **Objective Modules (Listening & Reading)**: 40 questions each. Graded strictly against standardized answer keys. No examiner discretion.
- **Subjective Modules (Writing & Speaking)**: Evaluated by accredited Cambridge examiners using four 9-band analytical descriptors.

### Component Architecture & Timing Matrix

| Skill | Timing | Structure | Input Format | Scoring Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Listening** | 30 min audio (+10 min transfer PBT / +2 min review CBT) | 4 Sections, 40 items | Social dialogue $\to$ Solo monologue $\to$ Academic dialogue $\to$ Academic lecture | Objective (Raw score /40 $\to$ Band 1-9) |
| **Reading** | 60 min (no transfer time) | 3 Passages, 40 items | Progressive difficulty (Passage 1 $\to$ 3) | Objective (Raw score /40 $\to$ Band 1-9) |
| **Writing** | 60 min | Task 1 (20 min, 150+ words) & Task 2 (40 min, 250+ words) | Task 1 visual report + Task 2 discursive essay | Subjective (TR/TA, CC, LR, GRA) |
| **Speaking** | 11–14 min | Part 1 (Interview), Part 2 (Long Turn), Part 3 (Discussion) | Face-to-face with certified examiner | Subjective (FC, LR, GRA, PR) |

### Band Score Conversion Guidelines (Academic & General)

| Target Band | Academic Listening (/40) | Academic Reading (/40) | General Training Reading (/40) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Band 6.0** | 23–25 | 23–26 | 30–31 |
| **Band 6.5** | 26–29 | 27–29 | 32–33 |
| **Band 7.0** | 30–31 | 30–32 | 34–35 |
| **Band 7.5** | 32–34 | 33–34 | 36 |
| **Band 8.0** | 35–36 | 35–36 | 37–38 |
| **Band 8.5** | 37–38 | 37–38 | 39 |
| **Band 9.0** | 39–40 | 39–40 | 40 |

### Delivery Modalities: Computer-Delivered (CBT) vs. Paper-Based (PBT)
- CBT and PBT share identical question types, assessment criteria, difficulty, and speaking interviews.
- **CBT Specifics**: Audio delivered via high-fidelity individual headphones; typing with live word counter in Writing; split-screen scrolling in Reading; 2-minute review period at end of Listening.
- **One Skill Retake (OSR)**: Standardized for CBT candidates across certified test centers. Allows single-skill retake within 60 days of the primary sitting.

## Section 3: Grammar and Vocabulary Diagnostic Tests

The diagnostic suite assesses baseline accuracy across high-frequency error vectors that systematically trap candidates below Band 7.0.

### Diagnostic Question Bank (20 Items)

#### Question 1
- **ID**: `diag-01`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "The rapid expansion of urban renewable energy grids _____ significant capital investments from local authorities."
- **Options**:
  - A) requires
  - B) require
  - C) are requiring
  - D) have required
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: The subject is the singular noun 'expansion', not 'energy grids'. A singular subject takes the singular verb 'requires'.
- **Impact on Band**: Subject-verb agreement errors with long noun phrases keep Grammatical Range and Accuracy below Band 7.0.

#### Question 2
- **ID**: `diag-02`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "Since the 1990s, international student mobility _____ exponentially across OECD nations."
- **Options**:
  - A) increased
  - B) is increasing
  - C) had increased
  - D) has increased
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: 'Since the 1990s' describes a trend starting in the past and continuing to the present, which takes the present perfect 'has increased'.
- **Impact on Band**: Using the past simple for ongoing trends limits Grammatical Range and Accuracy to Band 6.0.

#### Question 3
- **ID**: `diag-03`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `articles`
- **Prompt**: "Access to _____ clean drinking water is essential for public health in rural communities."
- **Options**:
  - A) the
  - B) an
  - C) Ø (no article)
  - D) a
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Clean drinking water' is an uncountable noun used generally here, so it takes no article (Ø). Adding 'the' would point to a specific water supply.
- **Impact on Band**: Article mistakes across an essay restrict Lexical Resource and Grammatical Accuracy to Band 6.0.

#### Question 4
- **ID**: `diag-04`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "Although governments subsidize electric vehicles, _____ consumer adoption rates continue to lag behind initial forecasts."
- **Options**:
  - A) but
  - B) however
  - C) yet
  - D) Ø (no conjunction)
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: 'Although' already connects the two clauses. English sentences cannot pair 'Although' with 'but', 'yet', or 'however' in the main clause.
- **Impact on Band**: Pairing 'Although' with 'but' is a serious structural error that holds Grammatical Range and Accuracy to Band 5.5.

#### Question 5
- **ID**: `diag-05`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "There was a sharp decline _____ the proportion of households relying exclusively on fossil fuel heating."
- **Options**:
  - A) in
  - B) of
  - C) to
  - D) on
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: Nouns of change like 'decline', 'rise', or 'drop' take 'in' before the metric that changed ('a decline in proportion').
- **Impact on Band**: Wrong prepositions in Task 1 trend descriptions weaken both Lexical Resource and Grammatical Accuracy.

#### Question 6
- **ID**: `diag-06`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Rigorous laboratory analysis must _____ significant influence over the validity of research conclusions."
- **Options**:
  - A) wield
  - B) manifest
  - C) derive
  - D) exert
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: The natural pairing is 'exert influence'. You wield power, manifest symptoms, and derive benefits, but influence pairs with exert.
- **Impact on Band**: Accurate verb-noun collocations are essential for reaching Band 7.0 or higher in Lexical Resource.

#### Question 7
- **ID**: `diag-07`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "Neither the municipal council nor the transport ministry _____ prepared to fund the suburban railway expansion."
- **Options**:
  - A) were
  - B) have been
  - C) are
  - D) was
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: With 'neither... nor...', the verb agrees with the closer subject ('the transport ministry'), which is singular, so use 'was'.
- **Impact on Band**: Handling proximity agreement accurately shows the grammatical control needed for Band 8.0.

#### Question 8
- **ID**: `diag-08`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "By the time modern carbon-capture facilities become operational in 2035, global average temperatures _____ by 1.2 degrees Celsius."
- **Options**:
  - A) will rise
  - B) would rise
  - C) will have risen
  - D) has risen
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'By the time' combined with a future year points to an action finished before that point, requiring the future perfect 'will have risen'.
- **Impact on Band**: Using the future perfect correctly for future deadlines demonstrates Band 8.0 grammatical range.

#### Question 9
- **ID**: `diag-09`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "The researcher examined forty clinical trials, the majority of _____ exhibited methodological inconsistencies."
- **Options**:
  - A) which
  - B) them
  - C) that
  - D) whom
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: After a quantifier like 'the majority of' linking two clauses, use the relative pronoun 'which'. Using 'them' without a conjunction creates a comma splice.
- **Impact on Band**: Comma splices reduce scores in both Coherence & Cohesion and Grammatical Accuracy.

#### Question 10
- **ID**: `diag-10`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `articles`
- **Prompt**: "The survey revealed that _____ unemployed face distinct psychological barriers when seeking reintegration."
- **Options**:
  - A) all
  - B) these
  - C) Ø
  - D) the
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: 'The' plus an adjective ('the unemployed') refers to the group as a whole and functions as a plural noun.
- **Impact on Band**: Using group adjectives with 'the' demonstrates the syntactic variety expected at Band 7.5 and above.

#### Question 11
- **ID**: `diag-11`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Technological automation poses a _____ threat to traditional clerical vocations."
- **Options**:
  - A) acute
  - B) critical
  - C) grave
  - D) pronounced
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Grave threat' is the standard collocation for a serious danger. 'Acute' and 'critical' pair with problems or situations, but not with threats.
- **Impact on Band**: Choosing the exact collocate separates Band 7.0 from Band 8.0 lexical precision.

#### Question 12
- **ID**: `diag-12`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "Rarely _____ such extensive economic restructuring without significant transitory unemployment."
- **Options**:
  - A) nations undertake
  - B) do nations undertake
  - C) are nations undertaking
  - D) nations have undertaken
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: Placing 'Rarely' at the start of a sentence triggers inversion: auxiliary 'do' + subject + base verb 'undertake'.
- **Impact on Band**: Accurate negative inversion demonstrates high grammatical range for Band 8.0 and above.

#### Question 13
- **ID**: `diag-13`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "The government's new fiscal policy is aimed _____ mitigating regional economic disparities."
- **Options**:
  - A) to
  - B) for
  - C) at
  - D) on
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Aimed' takes the preposition 'at' followed by an -ing verb ('aimed at mitigating').
- **Impact on Band**: Mistakes with dependent prepositions weaken sentence accuracy and cohesion in Task 2.

#### Question 14
- **ID**: `diag-14`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "To address adolescent obesity, schools must _____ proactive measures to encourage physical activity."
- **Options**:
  - A) make
  - B) take
  - C) do
  - D) execute
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: English uses 'take measures' or 'adopt measures', never 'make measures'.
- **Impact on Band**: Confusing common verbs like 'make' and 'take' prevents reaching Band 7.0 in Lexical Resource.

#### Question 15
- **ID**: `diag-15`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "A substantial proportion of the arable land in arid zones _____ affected by desertification."
- **Options**:
  - A) are
  - B) is
  - C) have been
  - D) were
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: With fractions or proportions, verb agreement follows the noun after 'of'. Because 'arable land' is uncountable, it takes the singular verb 'is'.
- **Impact on Band**: Proportional agreement is essential for accurate descriptions in Task 1 reports.

#### Question 16
- **ID**: `diag-16`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "Had the regulatory framework been introduced in 2006, the 2008 financial crisis _____ significantly mitigated."
- **Options**:
  - A) would be
  - B) will have been
  - C) would have been
  - D) was
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: An inverted third conditional ('Had the framework been...') needs 'would have been' in the main clause to describe a counterfactual past outcome.
- **Impact on Band**: Hypothetical conditionals like this show the complex sentence range needed for Band 8.0.

#### Question 17
- **ID**: `diag-17`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "The empirical data failed to _____ the hypothesis proposed by the research team."
- **Options**:
  - A) ratify
  - B) corroborate
  - C) authorize
  - D) certify
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: Data can 'corroborate a hypothesis' (support it with evidence). 'Ratify', 'authorize', and 'certify' apply to laws, permits, or credentials, not scientific claims.
- **Impact on Band**: Using specific research vocabulary helps raise Lexical Resource to Band 8.0.

#### Question 18
- **ID**: `diag-18`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "The curriculum was redesigned, _____ students with practical vocational competencies."
- **Options**:
  - A) thereby provided
  - B) thereby providing
  - C) and thereby provide
  - D) which thereby provide
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: 'Thereby' followed by an -ing participle ('thereby providing') forms a result clause that connects the action to its effect.
- **Impact on Band**: Participle clauses of result add syntactic variety for Band 8.0 Grammatical Range.

#### Question 19
- **ID**: `diag-19`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "Expenditure on healthcare accounted _____ approximately 18% of the national budget."
- **Options**:
  - A) of
  - B) with
  - C) for
  - D) to
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Account for' is the standard phrase for representing a share or percentage ('accounted for 18%').
- **Impact on Band**: Accurate proportion verbs are essential in Task 1 reports to avoid penalties in Task Achievement and grammar.

#### Question 20
- **ID**: `diag-20`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Rapid industrialization has led to _____ environmental degradation across developing corridors."
- **Options**:
  - A) unprecedented
  - B) unintended
  - C) unwelcome
  - D) uncontrolled
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: 'Unprecedented degradation' means environmental damage on a scale never seen before. 'Unintended' and 'unwelcome' describe unwanted effects, but do not convey the historical scale of 'unprecedented'.
- **Impact on Band**: Choosing adjectives that capture exact scale reflects Band 8.0 lexical precision.

| Score (/20) | Baseline Level | Primary Remediation Focus | Action Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 – 11** | Below Band 6.0 | Morphosyntactic foundations: Subject-verb concord, article zero/definite rules, sentence boundary mechanics | Prioritize Grammar Diagnostic Drills & review basic clausal structures before attempting full essays. |
| **12 – 15** | Band 6.0 – 6.5 | Syntactic complexity & academic register: Eliminating comma splices, mastering dependent prepositions, expanding collocations | Focus on 4-Sentence Paragraph Workbench and academic collocation tables. |
| **16 – 18** | Band 7.0 – 7.5 | Advanced clausal control: Inversions, participle result clauses, modal hedging | Refine task-specific precision, transition matrices, and lexical nuances. |
| **19 – 20** | Band 8.0 – 9.0 | Near-native flexibility: Rare grammatical structures, idiomatic academic phrasing, stylistic economy | Maximize simulation speed and focus on high-stakes argumentative defense. |
