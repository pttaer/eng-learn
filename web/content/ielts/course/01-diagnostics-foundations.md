# Module I: Foundations & Diagnostics

## Section 1: General Overview & Foundations

### Course Overview & Core Pedagogical Pillars
The **IELTS Band 7+ Complete Prep Course** is structured around reverse-engineered examiner rubrics. Reaching Band 7.0–8.5+ requires moving beyond passive conversational English and executing rigorous academic discourse structures under strict timing conditions.

1. **Rubric-First Calibration**: Every strategy directly targets official IELTS criteria:
   - **Writing**: Task Achievement / Task Response (25%), Coherence & Cohesion (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%).
   - **Speaking**: Fluency & Coherence (25%), Lexical Resource (25%), Grammatical Range & Accuracy (25%), Pronunciation (25%).
2. **Unified Organization Method**: Consistent, repeatable formulas for every writing task and speaking response, eliminating uncertainty on exam day.
3. **Keyword & Paraphrasing Engine**: Recognizing synonym pairs, morphological shifts, and antonyms under intense time constraints.
4. **Distractor Neutralization**: Proactively spotting and discarding examiner traps (qualifiers, absolute statements, negative reversals like *however*, *on the other hand*, *actually*).

### Strategic Study Schedules
- **4-Week Intensive Schedule**: 3–4 hours/day. Alternates Writing Task 2 + Speaking on Days 1, 3, 5 with Reading + Listening + Writing Task 1 on Days 2, 4, 6.
- **8-Week Standard Schedule**: 1.5–2 hours/day. Dedicated single skill deep dive per week with cumulative full-length timed review every weekend.
- **Master Rule**: Never attempt timed mock simulations until achieving 80%+ accuracy in un-timed tactical application drills.

## Section 2: General Information & Exam Architecture

### Objective vs. Subjective Assessment
- **Objective Modules (Listening & Reading)**: 40 questions each. Graded strictly against standardized answer keys. No examiner discretion.
- **Subjective Modules (Writing & Speaking)**: Evaluated by accredited Cambridge examiners using four 9-band analytical descriptors.

### Component Architecture & Timing Matrix

| Skill | Timing | Structure | Input Format | Scoring Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Listening** | 30 min audio (+10 min transfer PBT / +2 min review CBT) | 4 Sections, 40 items | Social dialogue $\to$ Solo monologue $\to$ Academic dialogue $\to$ Academic lecture | Objective (Raw score /40 $\to$ Band 1-9) |
| **Reading** | 60 min (no transfer time) | 3 Passages, 40 items | Progressive difficulty (Passage 1 $\to$ 3) | Objective (Raw score /40 $\to$ Band 1-9) |
| **Writing** | 60 min | Task 1 (20 min, 150+ words) & Task 2 (40 min, 250+ words) | Task 1 visual report + Task 2 discursive essay | Subjective (TR/TA, CC, LR, GRA) |
| **Speaking** | 11–14 min | Part 1 (Interview), Part 2 (Long Turn), Part 3 (Discussion) | Face-to-face with certified examiner | Subjective (FC, LR, GRA, PR) |

### Band Score Conversion Guidelines (Academic & General)

| Target Band | Academic Listening (/40) | Academic Reading (/40) | General Training Reading (/40) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Band 6.0** | 23–25 | 23–26 | 30–31 |
| **Band 6.5** | 26–29 | 27–29 | 32–33 |
| **Band 7.0** | 30–31 | 30–32 | 34–35 |
| **Band 7.5** | 32–34 | 33–34 | 36 |
| **Band 8.0** | 35–36 | 35–36 | 37–38 |
| **Band 8.5** | 37–38 | 37–38 | 39 |
| **Band 9.0** | 39–40 | 39–40 | 40 |

### Delivery Modalities: Computer-Delivered (CBT) vs. Paper-Based (PBT)
- CBT and PBT share identical question types, assessment criteria, difficulty, and speaking interviews.
- **CBT Specifics**: Audio delivered via high-fidelity individual headphones; typing with live word counter in Writing; split-screen scrolling in Reading; 2-minute review period at end of Listening.
- **One Skill Retake (OSR)**: Standardized for CBT candidates across certified test centers. Allows single-skill retake within 60 days of the primary sitting.

## Section 3: Grammar and Vocabulary Diagnostic Tests

The diagnostic suite assesses baseline accuracy across high-frequency error vectors that systematically trap candidates below Band 7.0.

### Diagnostic Question Bank (20 Items)

#### Question 1
- **ID**: `diag-01`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "The rapid expansion of urban renewable energy grids _____ significant capital investments from local authorities."
- **Options**:
  - A) requires
  - B) require
  - C) are requiring
  - D) have required
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: The subject of the sentence is the singular head noun 'expansion', not the plural prepositional adjunct 'energy grids'. Singular subjects demand the third-person singular verb 'requires'.
- **Impact on Band**: Prevents Band 7.0 GRA due to systematic subject-verb agreement slips with post-modified noun phrases.

#### Question 2
- **ID**: `diag-02`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "Since the 1990s, international student mobility _____ exponentially across OECD nations."
- **Options**:
  - A) increased
  - B) is increasing
  - C) had increased
  - D) has increased
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: The time marker 'Since the 1990s' anchors a continuous interval from a past point to the present, requiring the present perfect tense 'has increased'. Past simple incorrectly seals the action in the past.
- **Impact on Band**: Caps GRA at Band 6.0 if past simple is incorrectly substituted for continuous historical trends.

#### Question 3
- **ID**: `diag-03`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `articles`
- **Prompt**: "Access to _____ clean drinking water is essential for public health in rural communities."
- **Options**:
  - A) the
  - B) an
  - C) Ø (no article)
  - D) a
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Clean drinking water' is an uncountable noun used in a general sense, requiring the zero article (Ø). Using 'the' here is an erroneous over-generalization that restricts the meaning to a specific water source.
- **Impact on Band**: Systematic article overuse or omission caps Lexical Resource and Grammatical Accuracy at Band 6.0.

#### Question 4
- **ID**: `diag-04`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "Although governments subsidize electric vehicles, _____ consumer adoption rates continue to lag behind initial forecasts."
- **Options**:
  - A) but
  - B) however
  - C) yet
  - D) Ø (no conjunction)
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: 'Although' is a subordinating conjunction introducing a dependent concession clause. English syntax prohibits a paired coordinating conjunction ('but', 'yet') or conjunctive adverb ('however') in the main clause.
- **Impact on Band**: Double-conjunction structures ('Although... but...') represent a severe fossilized syntax error capping GRA at Band 5.5.

#### Question 5
- **ID**: `diag-05`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "There was a sharp decline _____ the proportion of households relying exclusively on fossil fuel heating."
- **Options**:
  - A) in
  - B) of
  - C) to
  - D) on
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: Trend nouns indicating rise, fall, increase, or decline take the preposition 'in' when followed by the metric or variable undergoing the change (e.g., 'a decline in proportion').
- **Impact on Band**: Misselecting prepositions in Task 1 trend descriptions destroys precision and penalizes Lexical Resource and GRA.

#### Question 6
- **ID**: `diag-06`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Rigorous laboratory analysis must _____ significant influence over the validity of research conclusions."
- **Options**:
  - A) wield
  - B) manifest
  - C) derive
  - D) exert
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: The verb 'exert' forms the precise academic collocation with 'influence' ('exert influence'). 'Wield' collocates with power; 'manifest' with symptoms; 'derive' with benefits, none matching 'influence' in formal discourse.
- **Impact on Band**: Band 7.0+ Lexical Resource requires using sophisticated collocations with precise verb-noun pairing in academic register.

#### Question 7
- **ID**: `diag-07`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "Neither the municipal council nor the transport ministry _____ prepared to fund the suburban railway expansion."
- **Options**:
  - A) were
  - B) have been
  - C) are
  - D) was
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: In correlative constructions with 'neither... nor...', the verb agrees with the closer subject ('the transport ministry', singular), hence 'was'.
- **Impact on Band**: Proves mastery of advanced proximity agreement rules required for Band 8.0 GRA.

#### Question 8
- **ID**: `diag-08`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "By the time modern carbon-capture facilities become operational in 2035, global average temperatures _____ by 1.2 degrees Celsius."
- **Options**:
  - A) will rise
  - B) would rise
  - C) will have risen
  - D) has risen
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: The future time anchor 'By the time [present verb] ... in 2035' mandates the future perfect tense 'will have risen' to indicate completion prior to that future point. Simple future 'will rise' ignores the prospective aspect.
- **Impact on Band**: Demonstrates command of complex temporal frameworks necessary for Band 8.0+ grammatical range.

#### Question 9
- **ID**: `diag-09`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "The researcher examined forty clinical trials, the majority of _____ exhibited methodological inconsistencies."
- **Options**:
  - A) which
  - B) them
  - C) that
  - D) whom
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: A non-restrictive relative clause following a quantifier phrase ('the majority of') referring to inanimate things ('trials') requires the relative pronoun 'which'. Using 'them' creates a comma splice.
- **Impact on Band**: Comma splices and run-on sentences restrict Coherence & Cohesion and GRA to Band 6.0.

#### Question 10
- **ID**: `diag-10`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `articles`
- **Prompt**: "The survey revealed that _____ unemployed face distinct psychological barriers when seeking reintegration."
- **Options**:
  - A) all
  - B) these
  - C) Ø
  - D) the
- **Correct**: 3
- **Explanation**: The definite article 'the' combined with an adjective ('unemployed') functions as a nominalized plural collective noun referring to the whole class of individuals. Neither quantifiers nor demonstratives work here.
- **Impact on Band**: Accurate usage of nominalized adjectives reflects high-level syntactic dexterity (Band 7.5+ GRA).

#### Question 11
- **ID**: `diag-11`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Technological automation poses a _____ threat to traditional clerical vocations."
- **Options**:
  - A) acute
  - B) critical
  - C) grave
  - D) pronounced
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: 'Grave threat' is the high-level academic collocation denoting severe and imminent peril. 'Acute' and 'critical' collocate with issues/situations but not threats; 'pronounced' suggests obvious but not threatening.
- **Impact on Band**: Distinguishes high-register academic collocations from conversational near-synonyms; separates Band 7.0 from Band 8.0 lexical precision.

#### Question 12
- **ID**: `diag-12`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "Rarely _____ such extensive economic restructuring without significant transitory unemployment."
- **Options**:
  - A) nations undertake
  - B) do nations undertake
  - C) are nations undertaking
  - D) nations have undertaken
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: Negative or restrictive adverbs ('rarely', 'seldom', 'hardly') at the beginning of a clause trigger subject-auxiliary inversion with the appropriate auxiliary ('do nations undertake'). Non-inverted and continuous structures fail the inversion rule.
- **Impact on Band**: Controlled negative inversion demonstrates Band 8.5+ grammatical range.

#### Question 13
- **ID**: `diag-13`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "The government's new fiscal policy is aimed _____ mitigating regional economic disparities."
- **Options**:
  - A) to
  - B) for
  - C) at
  - D) on
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: The adjective/passive participle 'aimed' collocated with an objective takes the preposition 'at' + gerund ('aimed at mitigating').
- **Impact on Band**: Dependent preposition inaccuracies undermine accuracy and cohesion in Task 2 essays.

#### Question 14
- **ID**: `diag-14`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "To address adolescent obesity, schools must _____ proactive measures to encourage physical activity."
- **Options**:
  - A) make
  - B) take
  - C) do
  - D) execute
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: The canonical academic collocation is 'take measures' or 'adopt measures'. Using 'make measures' is a direct L1 translation error.
- **Impact on Band**: Basic delexical verb substitution errors ('make' vs 'take') prevent attaining Band 7.0 in Lexical Resource.

#### Question 15
- **ID**: `diag-15`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `subject_verb`
- **Prompt**: "A substantial proportion of the arable land in arid zones _____ affected by desertification."
- **Options**:
  - A) are
  - B) is
  - C) have been
  - D) were
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: With fraction and proportion expressions, agreement is determined by the noun in the prepositional phrase ('arable land', uncountable singular), requiring the singular verb 'is'.
- **Impact on Band**: Demonstrates nuanced understanding of proportional concord essential for Task 1 report writing.

#### Question 16
- **ID**: `diag-16`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `tenses`
- **Prompt**: "Had the regulatory framework been introduced in 2006, the 2008 financial crisis _____ significantly mitigated."
- **Options**:
  - A) would be
  - B) will have been
  - C) would have been
  - D) was
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: Third conditional inverted structure ('Had [subject] been...') requires the past conditional modal perfect in the main clause ('would have been mitigated'). The past anchor clarifies that the consequence is counterfactual to actual history.
- **Impact on Band**: Accurate hypothetical reasoning is a hallmark of Band 8.0+ Task 2 essay arguments.

#### Question 17
- **ID**: `diag-17`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "The empirical data failed to _____ the hypothesis proposed by the research team."
- **Options**:
  - A) ratify
  - B) corroborate
  - C) authorize
  - D) certify
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: 'Corroborate the hypothesis' is the precise academic collocation meaning to support with evidence. 'Ratify', 'authorize', and 'certify' collocate with official agreements or credentials, not hypotheses.
- **Impact on Band**: Elevates Lexical Resource to Band 8.0 by utilizing precise academic register in formal academic discourse.

#### Question 18
- **ID**: `diag-18`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `clauses`
- **Prompt**: "The curriculum was redesigned, _____ students with practical vocational competencies."
- **Options**:
  - A) thereby provided
  - B) thereby providing
  - C) and thereby provide
  - D) which thereby provide
- **Correct**: 1
- **Explanation**: The adverb 'thereby' followed by a present participle ('providing') creates an adverbial clause of result, smoothly linking the action to its consequence.
- **Impact on Band**: Participle clauses increase syntactic density and clausal complexity for Band 8.0 GRA.

#### Question 19
- **ID**: `diag-19`
- **Category**: `grammar`
- **SubType**: `prepositions`
- **Prompt**: "Expenditure on healthcare accounted _____ approximately 18% of the national budget."
- **Options**:
  - A) of
  - B) with
  - C) for
  - D) to
- **Correct**: 2
- **Explanation**: The phrasal verb 'account for' is the obligatory academic expression for representing a proportion or percentage in Task 1 reports.
- **Impact on Band**: Mandatory accuracy for Task 1 visual reports; incorrect prepositions lead to immediate score reductions in Task Achievement and GRA.

#### Question 20
- **ID**: `diag-20`
- **Category**: `vocabulary`
- **SubType**: `collocations`
- **Prompt**: "Rapid industrialization has led to _____ environmental degradation across developing corridors."
- **Options**:
  - A) unprecedented
  - B) unintended
  - C) unwelcome
  - D) uncontrolled
- **Correct**: 0
- **Explanation**: 'Unprecedented degradation' is the high-level academic collocation indicating a level or severity never before experienced. 'Unintended', 'unwelcome', and 'uncontrolled' describe consequences but lack the formal gravity of 'unprecedented'.
- **Impact on Band**: Demonstrates Band 8.0+ precision in selecting collocates that convey magnitude and historical significance in formal registers.
