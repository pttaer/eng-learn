## Section 2: Riverside Eco-Park & Community Food Forest Tour

**Context**: A public address by the park director, Elena Rossi, welcoming a group of volunteers and visitors to the opening of the Riverside Urban Agriculture and Wetlands Conservation Reserve.

### Audio Transcript
- **Director**: Good morning, volunteers and welcome to the Riverside Urban Agriculture and Wetlands Reserve! Before we hand out your volunteer gloves and planting kits, I would like to give you a brief orientation on our sanctuary's history, safety guidelines, and site layout.
- Originally, this twenty-hectare riverfront site was a decommissioned industrial rail depot. Through a six-year community soil regeneration campaign, we removed over twelve tons of heavy industrial slag and planted native mycorrhizal fungi and nitrogen-fixing clover. Today, it hosts over two hundred avian species and produces five metric tons of organic heirloom produce annually for local food banks.
- To protect nesting waterbirds, we strictly enforce three core park rules. First, dogs must remain leashed on designated perimeter gravel trails at all times; they are completely prohibited from entering the inner wetland boardwalk. Second, harvesting from the heritage fruit orchard is permitted only during guided Sunday workshops—please do not pluck fruit independently. Third, all single-use plastics are banned within the sanctuary; please refill your water bottles at our solar-powered rainwater filtration taps located behind the visitors' pavilion.
- Now, please take a look at your site map. We are currently standing at the Visitors' Welcome Pavilion, adjacent to the South Gate entrance. If you look directly to the north, across the central wildflower meadow, you will see a large timber-framed structure with a green sedum roof—that is our Permaculture Seed Bank and Laboratory, where our botanists store endangered native plant varieties.
- To your immediate right, heading east along the winding willow brook, lies the Children's Sensory Herb Garden, which features raised tactile beds with mint, lavender, and culinary herbs. If you walk past the Sensory Garden towards the northeastern corner, you will find our newly constructed Solar Hydroponics Greenhouse, which produces leafy greens using 90% less water than traditional tillage.
- Finally, if you take the gravel path to your left, heading west toward the riverbend, you will cross the footbridge into our Wetland Bird Observatory. The observatory hide has been constructed using reclaimed railway timber, offering an uninterrupted view of the heron nesting lagoons. Please maintain quiet when approaching the hide so as not to disturb the migratory bitterns. Let us now divide into our morning volunteer planting teams!

### Questions 11–15: Multiple Choice
*Choose the correct letter, **A, B, or C**.*

- **Question 11**: What was the original historical use of the Riverside Eco-Park site?  
  - **A.** An agricultural cattle pasture  
  - **B.** A decommissioned railway depot  
  - **C.** A commercial chemical production factory  
  *Key*: `B`
- **Question 12**: What method was primarily used to remediate the industrial soil?
