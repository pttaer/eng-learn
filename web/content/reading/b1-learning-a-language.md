# Passage
Many adults think that they are too old to learn a new language, but I disagree. I started learning English at the age of twenty-five, and I have made good progress since then. It was not always easy, and sometimes I wanted to give up. However, I have learned that small habits are more important than big efforts.

The first thing I did was to study a little every day. Ten or fifteen minutes is enough if you keep doing it for months. I also listened to short podcasts on my way to work, and I wrote down new words in a notebook. After a few weeks, I noticed that I could understand more than before.

Making mistakes was the hardest part. At first, I was afraid that people would laugh at me, so I stayed quiet in class. Later, a teacher told me that mistakes are a normal part of learning. If you never make a mistake, you are probably not trying anything new. After that, I began to speak more, and my confidence slowly grew.

If you want to learn a language, my advice is simple. Choose a goal that is realistic, find something you enjoy, and do not compare yourself with other people. You will not become perfect in a month, but you will certainly improve if you keep going. Learning a language is a long journey, but it is worth it.

## Syntax Dissection
### Sentence 1
- **Sentence**: I started learning English at the age of twenty-five, and I have made good progress since then.
- **Subject**: I
- **Verb**: started; have made
- **Object**: learning English; good progress
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Coordinated Clause]: and I have made good progress since then | Shows the result up to now.
- **Analysis**: The past simple ("started") gives a finished starting point, and the present perfect ("have made") links that time to the present. "Since then" supports the present perfect.

### Sentence 2
- **Sentence**: Ten or fifteen minutes is enough if you keep doing it for months.
- **Subject**: Ten or fifteen minutes
- **Verb**: is
- **Object**: (none; "enough" is the complement)
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Conditional Clause]: if you keep doing it for months | Gives the condition under which a short time is enough.
- **Analysis**: A period of time is treated as one unit, so the verb is singular ("is"). "Keep + -ing" means to continue.

### Sentence 3
- **Sentence**: At first, I was afraid that people would laugh at me, so I stayed quiet in class.
- **Subject**: I
- **Verb**: was; stayed
- **Object**: (none; "afraid" and "quiet" are complements)
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Noun Clause]: that people would laugh at me | Explains what the writer was afraid of; "would" shows a past fear about the future.
  - [Result Clause]: so I stayed quiet in class | Shows the result of the fear.
- **Analysis**: "Would" in a reported fear is the past form of "will". The linking word "so" joins the cause and the result.

### Sentence 4
- **Sentence**: If you never make a mistake, you are probably not trying anything new.
- **Subject**: you
- **Verb**: are (not) trying
- **Object**: anything new
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Conditional Clause]: If you never make a mistake | Describes a general situation, so the present simple is used.
- **Analysis**: A zero-type conditional states a general truth. The present continuous "are trying" talks about what you do in general now, and "probably" shows that the speaker is not completely sure.
