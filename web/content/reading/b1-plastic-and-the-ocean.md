# Passage
Plastic is cheap, light and useful, so we use it every day. We buy food in plastic boxes, we drink from plastic bottles, and we carry our shopping in plastic bags. The problem is that plastic does not disappear quickly. Much of it is thrown away after only one use, and some of it ends up in rivers and finally in the sea.

Plastic in the ocean can be dangerous for sea animals. Birds and fish sometimes mistake small pieces for food, and others get caught in old fishing nets. Over time, large pieces break into smaller ones, and these are very difficult to clean up. Scientists have been studying this problem for many years, and they agree that we need to act.

I believe that everyone can help, even in small ways. If we carried our own bags and bottles, we would use much less plastic. Many shops have already started to charge for plastic bags, and this has made people think before they buy. In some places, old bottles can be returned and recycled, which is a good idea.

Of course, individual action is not enough on its own. Governments and companies also have to take responsibility, because they make most of the decisions about packaging. However, if each of us makes better choices, the sea will have a better chance of staying clean. It is a problem we can all help to solve.

## Cold Read
- **Thesis**: Plastic is useful but it harms sea animals and lasts a long time, so both individuals and companies need to use less of it and recycle more.
- **Targets**: thrown away, ends up, mistake for, get caught, clean up, take responsibility, make a choice

### Comprehension Checks
- Q: Why is plastic a problem?
  A: It does not disappear quickly, and much of it is thrown away after one use and ends up in the sea.
- Q: How can plastic hurt sea animals?
  A: Animals sometimes mistake small pieces for food, or they get caught in old fishing nets.
- Q: What does the writer say about individual action?
  A: Everyone can help, but it is not enough alone because governments and companies also have to take responsibility.

## Syntax Dissection
### Sentence 1
- **Sentence**: Much of it is thrown away after only one use, and some of it ends up in rivers and finally in the sea.
- **Subject**: Much of it; some of it
- **Verb**: is thrown away; ends up
- **Object**: (none; both verbs are followed by adverbial phrases)
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Coordinated Clause]: and some of it ends up in rivers and finally in the sea | Adds the next step in the journey of plastic waste.
- **Analysis**: The first clause uses the passive ("is thrown away") because the person who throws it away is not important. The second clause uses the phrasal verb "ends up" to show the final result.

### Sentence 2
- **Sentence**: Birds and fish sometimes mistake small pieces for food, and others get caught in old fishing nets.
- **Subject**: Birds and fish; others
- **Verb**: mistake; get caught
- **Object**: small pieces (for food)
- **Analysis**: Two coordinated clauses with the same time meaning (present simple for general facts). "Mistake ... for" means to think something is something else, and "get caught" is a passive-like structure with "get".

### Sentence 3
- **Sentence**: Scientists have been studying this problem for many years, and they agree that we need to act.
- **Subject**: Scientists; they
- **Verb**: have been studying; agree
- **Object**: this problem
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Noun Clause]: that we need to act | Gives the content of what the scientists agree on.
- **Analysis**: The present perfect continuous ("have been studying") with "for many years" shows an activity that started in the past and continues now. The "that" clause follows the verb "agree".

### Sentence 4
- **Sentence**: If we carried our own bags and bottles, we would use much less plastic.
- **Subject**: we
- **Verb**: would use
- **Object**: much less plastic
- **Subordinate Clauses**:
  - [Second Conditional Clause]: If we carried our own bags and bottles | An imaginary situation, so the past simple is used.
- **Analysis**: A second conditional sentence suggests a possible change in behaviour. "Much less" is used with an uncountable noun.
