## 2. Pedagogical Core: CEFR Can-Do Communicative Action Tasks (A1–C2)

### 2.1 The Four Communicative Modes (CEFR 2020 CV)
Traditional ESL programs conflate "skills" into reading, writing, listening, and speaking. The 2020 CEFR Companion Volume replaces this with four communicative modes:
- **Reception**: Understanding audio, text, multimodal media (listening/reading comprehension).
- **Production**: Monologic oral presentation or written exposition (one-way, planned output).
- **Interaction**: Spoken or written co-construction of meaning in real time (bilateral, turn-taking, repair).
- **Mediation**: Acting as a linguistic and conceptual bridge (summarizing, explaining data, relaying key information, facilitating plurilingual collaboration).

## 3. L1 Vietnamese Interference Remediation Engine

Vietnamese learners do not make errors randomly. Errors are predictable systemic transfers resulting from typological differences between Austroasiatic/Vietic and Indo-European linguistic structures.

### 3.1 The 4 Primary Fossilization Vectors

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            THE 4 L1 VIETNAMESE INTERFERENCE VECTORS                             |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. PHONOLOGICAL & MORPHOLOGICAL    | Syllable structure (C)(w)V(C) lacking consonant clusters    |
|    Final Consonant Deletion        | and final fricatives/plosives -> Dropped -s, -ed, endings.   |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. SYNTACTIC PREDICATION           | Adjectives function as statutory predicates without copula  |
|    Zero-Copula & Be-Overuse        | ("Cô ấy đẹp") -> "She very beautiful" / "I am agree".       |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. TEMPORAL MECHANICS              | Tense marked by external optional particles (đã, đang, sẽ)  |
|    Tense vs. Aspect Particles      | or context -> Time word present causes verb inflection drop. |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4. NOMINAL & CLASSIFIER LOGIC      | Nouns lack grammatical number; classified by "cái, con"     |
|    Count/Uncount & Article Gaps    | -> Plural -s omission, "many advices/informations".        |
+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

### Vector 1: Final Consonant Deletion & Morpho-Phonemic Neutralization

#### Linguistic Root Cause:
In Vietnamese, syllables follow a rigid `(C)(w)V(C)` template. The only permissible coda consonants are unreleased stops `/p, t, k/`, nasals `/m, n, ŋ/`, and semivowels `/j, w/`. Crucially:
- No fricatives `/s, z, f, v, θ, ð, ʃ, ʒ/` or affricates `/tʃ, dʒ/` can ever occur in the syllable coda.
- Vietnamese has zero consonant clusters (both onset and coda).
- No consonant may follow a diphthong (e.g., *night* /naɪt/, *rice* /raɪs/ become /naɪ/, /raɪ/).
- Consequently, English inflectional endings (plural `-s/-es`, 3rd person singular `-s/-es`, past tense `-ed`) and lexical distinction codas (*five, fight, fine, file*) are deleted or neutralized.

#### Contrastive Diagnosis:
- *Learner Output*: "My friend live in five big house."
- *Underlying Parse*: Friend[ø] live[ø] in fi[ø] big hou[ø].
- *Phonemic Trap*: $/faɪv/ \rightarrow /faɪ/$, $/haʊs/ \rightarrow /haʊ/$, $/lɪvz/ \rightarrow /lɪv/$.

#### Targeted Remediation Micro-Drills:

1. **"Finish the Word" Catenation Bridge (Linking Protocol)**:
   Vietnamese speakers drop final consonants because isolated stops are unreleased. Linking the coda consonant to a following vowel transforms the impossible coda into an intuitive onset consonant:
   - Step 1 (Disjoint): `nice` [naɪ] + `idea` [aɪ'dɪə] $\rightarrow$ failure.
   - Step 2 (Catenation): `nice‿idea` $\rightarrow$ `/naɪ.saɪ.'dɪə/` (Vietnamese speaker easily produces onset `/s/`).
   - Step 3 (Retrograde Isolation): `nice‿idea` $\rightarrow$ isolate `nice` with audible release.

2. **Acoustic Discrimination & Minimal Triad Grid**:
   Interactive 3-way forced choice identifying codas:
   - `/faɪv/` (five) vs. `/faɪt/` (fight) vs. `/faɪn/` (fine)
   - `/raɪs/` (rice) vs. `/raɪd/` (ride) vs. `/raɪt/` (write)

3. **Morpho-Phonemic 3-Track Sorting Card**:
   - Track 1 (`-s` = /s/, /z/, /ɪz/): *books* /s/ vs. *dogs* /z/ vs. *busses* /ɪz/.
   - Track 2 (`-ed` = /t/, /d/, /ɪd/): *watched* /t/ vs. *played* /d/ vs. *wanted* /ɪd/.

### Vector 2: Zero-Copula & Adjectival Predication

#### Linguistic Root Cause:
In Vietnamese, descriptive adjectives belong to the class of stative verbs (tính từ là vị ngữ). The copula *là* is strictly reserved for nominal equative clauses (*Tôi là giáo viên* = I am a teacher). It is grammatically prohibited before adjectives (*Tôi là đẹp* is ungrammatical; the correct form is *Tôi đẹp*).
When Vietnamese learners translate thought into English:
- Stative adjectives need no linking verb: `Chị ấy rất bận` $\rightarrow$ *"She very busy"*.
- The room is clean $\rightarrow$ *"The room clean"*.
- Later, upon being repeatedly corrected that English sentences require *is/are/am*, learners overcompensate by mechanically prepending *be* before true lexical verbs: *"I am agree"*, *"She is work in a hospital"*, *"I am come from Vietnam"*.

#### Contrastive Diagnosis:
- *State 1 (Zero-Copula)*: Subject + [Adjective-as-Verb] $\rightarrow$ *"My sister very tall."*
- *State 2 (Hyper-Correction)*: Subject + Copula + [Lexical Verb] $\rightarrow$ *"I am agree with your opinion."*

#### Targeted Remediation Micro-Drills:

1. **State vs. Action Syntactic Sieve**:
   A visual card highlighting the grammatical necessity of the copula:
   - `[State / Quality]` $\rightarrow$ Requires Copula Engine (`AM / IS / ARE` + Adjective):
     * *Incorrect*: "The weather very hot."
     * *Correct*: "The weather **is** very hot."
   - `[Lexical Action / Mental Verb]` $\rightarrow$ Pure Verb (Zero Copula):
     * *Incorrect*: "I am agree."
     * *Correct*: "I **agree**." (Agree is an action verb in English, not an adjective).

2. **Vietnamese "Rất" Replacement Protocol**:
   Vietnamese uses *rất* directly before verbs (*Tôi rất thích*). In English, *very* cannot modify verbs directly.
   - *Error*: "I very like reading books."
   - *Transformation Drill*:
     * *Option A*: "I **really** like reading books."
     * *Option B*: "I like reading books **very much**."

### Vector 3: Tense vs. Aspect Particles (Temporal Independence)

#### Linguistic Root Cause:
Vietnamese has no grammatical tense inflection (inflectional morphology is zero). Time reference is established through:
1. Context or explicit time adverbials (*Hôm qua* = yesterday, *Ngày mai* = tomorrow).
2. Optional aspect particles: *đã* (anterior aspect / already occurred), *đang* (progressive aspect / in progress), *sẽ* (prospective aspect / future intent), *rồi* (completive), *chưa* (incompletive).
If a temporal adverbial is present, the particle is omitted because the time is already clear: *Hôm qua tôi đi chợ* (Yesterday I go market).
In English, finite clauses **must** mark tense on the verb regardless of whether a time word is present. Furthermore, Vietnamese *rồi* conflates Past Simple and Present Perfect, leading to the ubiquitous *"I eat already"* construction.

#### Contrastive Diagnosis:
- *Error 1 (Adverbial Tense Drop)*: "Last week my manager meet the client." (Adverbial *last week* satisfies temporal marking in learner's mind; inflecting *meet* $\rightarrow$ *met* feels redundant).
- *Error 2 (Aspect Conflation with 'Already')*: "I live here already 5 years." (Direct transfer of *Tôi sống ở đây 5 năm rồi*).

#### Targeted Remediation Micro-Drills:

1. **The "Dual-Anchor" Time Protocol**:
   Rule: Every English past-time sentence requires **two anchors**:
   - Anchor 1: When it happened (Time expression: *Yesterday, In 2024, Last night*).
   - Anchor 2: The Verb's Past Armor (Morphological form: *went, saw, wrote, decided*).
   - If either anchor is missing, the sentence collapses.

2. **The "Rồi" Dichotomy Timeline**:
   Visual timeline distinguishing completed past vs. present relevance:
   - Event completely finished at specific past time $\rightarrow$ **Past Simple**:
     * *VN*: "Tôi làm xong báo cáo rồi (hồi 2 giờ)." $\rightarrow$ "I finished the report at 2 PM."
   - Event finished with current result / experience $\rightarrow$ **Present Perfect**:
     * *VN*: "Tôi làm xong báo cáo rồi (bây giờ bạn xem được rồi)." $\rightarrow$ "I have already finished the report."
   - Duration from past continuing to now $\rightarrow$ **Present Perfect Continuous**:
     * *VN*: "Tôi sống ở đây 5 năm rồi." $\rightarrow$ "I have lived / have been living here for 5 years." (NOT: *I live here 5 years already*).

### Vector 4: Countable / Uncountable & Classifier Transfer

#### Linguistic Root Cause:
Vietnamese nouns are invariable root concepts with no plural suffixes. To count or individuate an entity, Vietnamese mandatory inserts **classifiers**:
- *con* (animals: *hai con chó* = two dogs)
- *cái* (inanimate objects: *ba cái bàn* = three tables)
- *quyển / cuốn* (volumes: *bốn quyển sách* = four books)
Mass concepts like *tin tức* (news), *lời khuyên* (advice), *thông tin* (information), *bài tập* (homework) are counted using classifiers (*một mẩu tin*, *hai lời khuyên*).
When encountering English:
- Learners treat all English nouns as countable entities, affixing plural `-s` to non-count nouns: *"informations"*, *"advices"*, *"homeworks"*, *"equipments"*, *"furnitures"*.
- Conversely, singular count nouns are left bare without determiners: *"I have car"* (imitating *Tôi có xe*).

#### Contrastive Diagnosis:
- *Error 1*: "He gave me many useful advices and informations."
- *Error 2*: "She is teacher. I want to buy new computer."

#### Targeted Remediation Micro-Drills:

1. **Mass-Noun "Partitive Chunk" Matrix**:
   Teach uncountable nouns exclusively with their fixed lexical carriers:
   - Advice $\rightarrow$ *a piece of advice*, *words of advice*
   - Information $\rightarrow$ *a piece of information*, *a wealth of information*
   - Equipment $\rightarrow$ *a piece of equipment*, *a set of equipment*
   - Furniture $\rightarrow$ *an item of furniture*, *a suite of furniture*

2. **Singular Count Noun "No Naked Nouns" Sieve**:
   A singular countable noun in English **cannot stand alone**; it must wear an article or determiner:
   - [Bare / Naked]: *car*, *teacher*, *computer* $\rightarrow$ ILLEGAL.
   - [Dressed]: *a car*, *the teacher*, *my computer*, *this laptop* $\rightarrow$ LEGAL.

### 3.2 Secondary L1 Interference Traps & Remediation

| L1 Interference Phenomenon | Vietnamese Pattern | Erroneous English Output | Correct English Form | Pedagogical Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Existential "Có"** | *Ở Hà Nội có nhiều công viên.* (*Có* = have & there is) | *"In Hanoi has many parks."* | *"There are many parks in Hanoi."* | Drill *There is / There are* as a fixed existential trigger; reserve *have* for possession. |
| **Paired Connectors** | *Mặc dù... nhưng...* / *Vì... nên...* (Both halves required) | *"Although it rained, but we went out."* *"Because he was sick, so he stayed home."* | *"Although it rained, we went out."* *"Because he was sick, he stayed home."* | "One bridge, one toll" rule: English sentences only allow ONE subordinating conjunction per clause pair. |
| **Preposition Mismatch** | *nghe nhạc* (no prep), *thảo luận về* (*về* = about), *ở* (general) | *"listen music"*, *"discuss about"*, *"married with"* | *"listen to music"*, *"discuss the issue"*, *"married to"* | Teach verb + preposition as indivisible single lexical chunks (`listen-to`, `married-to`). |
| **Negative Question Agreement** | *Bạn không mệt à? — Vâng (không mệt).* (Agree with speaker) | Interlocutor: *"You didn't call him?"* Learner: *"Yes."* (meaning: *Yes, I didn't call*) | Interlocutor: *"You didn't call him?"* Learner: *"No, I didn't."* | In English, *Yes/No* reflects the objective fact (Did you call? No), never agreement with the speaker's negative syntax. |
| **Passive with Bị / Được** | *Tôi bị mất ví.* (*Bị* marks misfortune; active grammar) | *"I was lost my wallet."* *"I was stolen my bicycle."* | *"I lost my wallet."* *"My bicycle was stolen."* | Contrastive lesson: *Bị/được* expresses emotional valence in Vietnamese, whereas English passive is strictly syntactic agent-defocusing. |
